SynaptopathyDB integrates synaptic proteomes, genetic and phenotypic data to advance research on nervous system disorders

Sorokina O*, Dominic D*, Bayés À, Armstrong JD, Grant SGN

Scientific Reports · 2025 · Vol. 15 · 42986

Read abstract

Synaptic dysfunction resulting from pathogenic variants in genes encoding synaptic proteins is a major contributor to brain and behavioural disorders, collectively termed synaptopathies. To facilitate research into the genetic basis and clinical manifestations of synaptopathy we have created SynaptopathyDB, an online resource that integrates data from 64 mammalian synapse proteomic studies and multiple genetic and phenotypic resources (www.synaptopathyDB.org). We identified a consensus set of 3,437 mammalian synapse proteins from presynaptic and postsynaptic compartments, which have wide application in genetic and omic studies. Mutations in 954 genes encoding 28% of the consensus synapse proteome were associated with 1,266 OMIM diseases of the central and peripheral nervous system. We present findings that underscore the pervasive role of synaptic gene variants in the phenotypes of neurological, psychiatric, developmental, and systemic disorders highlighting the significant burden they impose on individuals and healthcare systems. SynaptopathyDB is a versatile platform and discovery tool for understanding the role of synapse proteins and genetic variants in human disease phenotypes.

A Brainwide Atlas of Synaptic Nanoarchitecture Across the Mouse Lifespan

Kaizuka T, et al. (including Dominic D, Qiu Z), Grant SGN

bioRxiv · 2026

Read abstract

This study introduces NanoSYNMAP, a genetic, optical and computational platform that combines FRET with synaptome mapping to measure nanoscale protein proximity within individual synapses across the whole mouse brain. Using it, the authors build the first brainwide atlas of synaptic nanoarchitecture, based on the proximity of postsynaptic MAGUK supercomplexes. Nanoarchitecture differs between brain regions, differentiates during postnatal development and remodels with ageing, and deletion of a neuropsychiatric risk gene triggers widespread reorganisation of synaptic nanoscale architecture.

The Overlap Area as a Novel Measure of Effect Size in Neuroscience Research

Wu Y, Woods H, Yuan K, Dominic D, Qiu Z, Grant SGN

bioRxiv · 2025

Read abstract

Traditional effect size measures such as Cohen's d focus on differences in means and overlook differences in variance between groups. This preprint proposes the overlap area (OA) between the population distributions of experimental and control groups as an effect size measure, estimated with a Bayesian approach, with significance thresholds for single and replicated experiments set by random resampling. Simulations support the method's sensitivity and robustness, and applied to a real dataset OA reveals the effects of environmental enrichment on the mouse brain synaptome. An open-source toolbox for OA analyses is released alongside the paper.

Experiences are encoded by brainwide reprogramming of synaptome architecture

Grant SGN, et al. (including Dominic D)

bioRxiv · 2025

Read abstract

This study reveals how experiences are encoded by brainwide reprogramming of synaptome architecture. Using synaptome mapping tools, the research demonstrates that experiences reshape the molecular and structural composition of synapses across the entire brain, providing new insights into how the brain stores and processes experiences at the synaptic level.

PRMix: Primary Region Mix Augmentation and Benchmark Dataset for Precise Whole Mouse Brain Anatomical Delineation

Yuan K, Woods H, Günar Ü, Dominic D, Wu Y, Qiu Z, Grant SGN

bioRxiv · 2025

Read abstract

The architecture of the mouse brain shares remarkable similarities with the human brain, making it an essential model for studying brain pathologies, synaptic diversity, and regional specialization. A key step in such studies involves registering molecular images to reference brain atlases, a process hindered by the difficulty of accurately delineating brain regions. Toward this, we have curated a collection of high-resolution, dual-fluorescence microscopy images, termed as dual-fluorescence mouse brain microscopy (DMBM) dataset, complemented by expert annotations of 118 subregions. This dataset provides unprecedented insights into the molecular and structural complexity of the mouse brain. However, its full potential for detailed whole-brain analysis is compromised by challenges such as boundary ambiguity and sample scarcity in existing automated segmentation methods, prompting the development of the primary region mix (PRMix) augmentation method. PRMix is specifically designed to expand these datasets, enhance the realism of synthetic data and minimize overlap between adjacent regions. Our approach, together with the curated dataset, achieves superior segmentation performance across the mouse brain compared with existing methods, setting a new benchmark in brain imaging research.
